Our first trip to China was Christmas 2025, and I thought I had prepared everything. Then we landed with no internet on my phone. What happened is just below.

This page is the setup list I wish I had before that trip. No sights, only the things that make the first day easy: entry rules, the arrival card, apps, paying, flights, trains, maps and packing. The video goes through the same 13 things. The facts were checked on 15 October 2026 against the sources at the bottom.

What went wrong on our first trip

On our first trip, Christmas 2025, my eSIM was ready, and I forgot to switch it on before we landed. So we did the thing I would not do again: we bought Chinese SIM cards at the airport. I also had only one VPN, NordVPN, and it needed wifi to activate again. In the end our hotel concierge shared the VPN he used, LetsVPN, and that got us back online.

When we went back for Easter 2026 we were fully prepared. My lesson from it: even when you research everything, have a backup plan.

Trains: I could not get through the 12306 registration in the Alipay version, so I booked all our train tickets on Trip.com and paid a small fee. If you want to use 12306 itself, download the real app from the App Store or Google Play.

Quick answers

Do I need a visa for China?

It depends on your passport. In October 2026 China listed 50 countries with visa-free entry for short stays, most of it dated to 31 December 2026. Check the official list for your own country before booking.

Which app do I need, Alipay or WeChat?

Both work for paying. Set both up at home, link a card to each, and you will not be stuck when one of them does not like your card.

Does Google Maps work in China?

No. Use Amap or Apple Maps, and keep the Chinese names of your hotel and stations saved offline.

When should I book high-speed trains?

Tickets go on sale about 15 days before the travel date. Book as soon as they open for busy routes.

Step by step

  1. 1

    Check the entry rules for your passport

    On 15 October 2026, China listed visa-free entry for passport holders from 50 countries, most of it running to 31 December 2026, and the usual advice is a passport valid for 6 more months. Check your own country on the official list before you book, because the dates move.

  2. 2

    Fill in the arrival card

    It is free and online. Do it on a computer or inside Alipay; both guides are on this site.

  3. 3

    Get the apps

    Alipay and WeChat, before you fly. Both have an English setup for visitors.

  4. 4

    Link your card

    Add your bank card to Alipay and WeChat Pay at home, while you still have your normal phone number and signal.

  5. 5

    Try a mini program

    Trains, taxis and the arrival card all live inside Alipay as mini programs. Open one at home so the first time is not at a station.

  6. 6

    Know the ¥200 line

    Foreign cards in Alipay and WeChat Pay carry a 3 percent fee on single payments above ¥200. Below that there is no fee, so small payments are cheap.

  7. 7

    Carry some cash and a backup card

    Not much is needed, but a little cash and a second card cover the one shop that cannot take your phone.

  8. 8

    Pick your flight

    From Ireland the options are a non-stop from Dublin or a one-stop through a hub. China Eastern now flies Dublin to Shanghai non-stop. We have flown to Shanghai via London and via Helsinki, and this December we go Cork, London, Chengdu. Check your own airport. To compare routes, there is a Kiwi.com link in Links for this guide.

  9. 9

    Book smart

    Book accommodation that confirms a real address, because the arrival card and any police registration both ask for it.

  10. 10

    Book trains in time

    High-speed train tickets go on sale about 15 days before travel. Book through the official 12306 app or Trip.com, and keep your passport number exactly as in the booking. I could not get through 12306 registration in the Alipay version, so I booked ours on Trip.com and paid a small fee.

  11. 11

    Station day

    Stations are big and often share a name with a city. Check the exact station on your ticket, arrive early, and have your passport out for the gate.

  12. 12

    Get maps that work

    Amap and Apple Maps work well in China; Google Maps does not. Save offline notes with the Chinese names of your hotel and stations.

  13. 13

    Pack light

    Trains and metros mean stairs and gates. One bag you can lift makes every station day easier.

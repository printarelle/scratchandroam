What I got wrong with apps the first time

On our first trip, Christmas 2025, my eSIM was ready, and I forgot to switch it on before we landed. So we did the thing I would not do again: we bought Chinese SIM cards at the airport. I also had only one VPN, NordVPN, and it needed wifi to activate again. In the end our hotel concierge shared the VPN he used, LetsVPN, and that got us back online.

When we went back for Easter 2026 we were fully prepared. My lesson from it: even when you research everything, have a backup plan.

Another time I pre-ordered a Nihao Mobile SIM and picked it up at the airport. The internet worked, and Meituan and food ordering worked. But a lot of other apps and attraction ticket sites kept refusing it, because the number counted as a virtual number. A full China Mobile SIM never gave me any of those errors.

Trains: I could not get through the 12306 registration in the Alipay version, so I booked all our train tickets on Trip.com and paid a small fee. If you want to use 12306 itself, download the real app from the App Store or Google Play.

Quick answers

Which apps do I need for paying, maps, taxis and trains in China?

Alipay and WeChat for paying, AMap Global for maps (Apple Maps also works on an iPhone), the DiDi taxi inside Alipay or AMap's own ride-hailing for taxis, and Railway 12306 or Trip.com for trains. With those set up you can get through almost any day.

Which China travel apps must be set up at home?

Anything that needs a passport check, a text code or a download from a store that is blocked in China: Alipay, WeChat, 12306, Trip.com, AMap, your eSIM and any VPN. Passport checks can take up to a few days and Google Play and VPN sites do not work once you land.

Which apps can I skip?

Google Maps, Uber, Airbnb for mainland stays, the standalone Meituan and Luckin apps, Alipay's Tour Card, Waygo, LetsVPN and any 12306 lookalike.

Do I need a separate taxi app in China?

No. DiDi opens inside Alipay under Transport, and AMap Global books rides in English. Download the DiDi app only if you prefer a standalone app.

Do I need a VPN if I have a travel eSIM?

Usually not on mobile data, because travel eSIM plans for China route your data abroad. A VPN still helps on hotel Wi-Fi, which does not, and it has to be installed before you fly.

The 12 apps, in the order to set them up

  1. 1

    Alipay: the one app you cannot skip

    Set up at home
    Download the international Alipay (on the App Store it is called Alipay - Simplify Your Life), verify your passport first under Me, Settings, Account and Security, Identity Information, then link a Visa or Mastercard. Do it two or three days before you fly: the passport check is usually minutes, but guides report waits of up to 72 hours, and the codes go by text to your home number. Payments up to ¥200 are free; a payment over ¥200 carries a 3% fee, so split big bills or pay those by card where you can. It comes first for one more reason: taxis, metro codes, bikes and food delivery already run inside it as mini-programs, so you can skip a lot of separate downloads (see the list below).

  2. 2

    WeChat and WeChat Pay: the backup with a 90-day fee break

    Set up at homeGood extra
    Small shops, scan-to-order menus and a lot of ticket systems run on WeChat, so have it ready even if Alipay is your main wallet. Add your card under Me, Pay and Services, Wallet, Bank Cards; any number that can get a text works, no Chinese number needed. New accounts are sometimes asked for a friend verification, where an existing user scans a code for you, which is one more reason to start at home. Good to know: Tencent announced on 27 May 2026 that first-time users who link an international card pay no transaction fee for 90 days from their first payment, on up to ¥1,000 a day. After that, WeChat's own help page says payments over ¥200 carry 3%.

  3. 3

    AMap Global: maps in English, with a taxi app hiding inside

    Set up at homeGood extra
    AMap (Gaode) is the map most people in China use, and the AMap Global app has had English since January 2025 and 14 more languages since July 2025. It also has its own ride-hailing that books several taxi companies at once and shows pickup points and fare estimates in English. Its August 2026 announcement says you can sign in with a phone number from 65 countries and regions and pay through Alipay or WeChat Pay, so link Alipay at home. Search tip: paste the Chinese name of your hotel from the booking. On an iPhone, Apple Maps also works in China without a VPN and is a fine second map.

  4. 4

    MetroMan China: an offline metro map for 53 cities

    Set up at homeGood extra
    A small English app with offline metro maps for 53 Chinese cities, including Chengdu, Chongqing and Xi'an; free, with an optional paid upgrade, and its App Store listing was updated on 30 September 2026. It is the one to open on a platform with no signal. You do not even need a ticket app for the metro in some cities: in Beijing, Shanghai and Chengdu you can tap a foreign Visa or Mastercard straight on the gate, and Alipay has a Transport code for each city you activate once.

  5. 5

    Railway 12306: the official train app

    Set up at homeGood extra
    The official China Railway app (on the App Store as 铁路12306, with English inside). Register with your passport; you cannot buy until it is verified, and verification can take a while, so do it at home. Tickets normally open 15 days ahead, counting the travel day. My own experience: I could not get through registration in the Alipay version of 12306, so I booked all our trains on Trip.com. If you want 12306, download the real app. Two features worth knowing: the waitlist for sold-out trains, where you prepay and the system keeps trying until shortly before departure and refunds you if it fails, and a 60-day reservation pilot running since 20 July 2026 on more than 200 Beijing to Shanghai trains, where you order 17 to 60 days ahead, the system fills orders 16 to 20 days before departure, and you pay by 23:00 that day. Also new: one free refund a day if you cancel within 30 minutes of buying and at least 4 hours before departure. Careful with lookalikes: China Train Ticket for 12306官网 is a private app, not the official one.

  6. 6

    Trip.com: the English backup for trains and hotels

    Set up at home
    The easiest place to book in English, with 24/7 English support and guest checkout for trains that does not need a verified passport. Its hotel search can filter for hotels that accept foreign guests, which matters because some hotels in China still cannot take foreigners. Trip.com's own pages disagree with each other about train booking fees in 2026, so compare the final price with 12306 before you pay.

  7. 7

    NIA 12367: the government app that does your arrival card

    Set up at homeGood extra
    The National Immigration Administration's app. Since 20 November 2025 you can fill in the China arrival card online before you land, in this app, in its Alipay or WeChat mini-program, or on the official website; Shanghai warns about lookalike scam sites, so use only official channels. It also handles police registration for stays outside hotels: hotels register you themselves, but if you stay in a flat or with friends you must register within 24 hours, and an online pilot started on 20 March 2026 in seven provinces including Sichuan and Chongqing. The Irish Department of Foreign Affairs notes that online registration needs a Chinese mobile number. Step by step: arrival card on a PC or in Alipay.

  8. 8

    A travel eSIM: how Google and WhatsApp keep working

    Set up at home
    eSIM sellers such as Airalo, Holafly, Nomad and Trip.com say their China plans route your data outside China, so Google, WhatsApp and Instagram work without a VPN; read the plan description to be sure. Install the eSIM at home on Wi-Fi: Airalo needs a connection to install, and Nomad says new plans cannot be bought once you are in mainland China. Two catches: an eSIM is data only, so keep your home SIM on for text codes from your bank, Alipay and WeChat, and hotel Wi-Fi does not route abroad, so blocked apps stop working the moment you switch to it.   9. 9

    A paid VPN, only as the backup for hotel Wi-Fi

    Set up at home
    You cannot get one after you land: Apple removed VPN apps from the China App Store in 2017 and provider websites are blocked. LetsVPN is the one our hotel concierge used to get us back online on our first trip. In April 2026 it announced it was ending its service for mainland China after weeks of blocking. Nobody publishes independent, dated tests from inside China, so pick a paid VPN with an obfuscation or stealth mode, install and test it at home, and treat it as a backup, not a plan. Using one sits in a legal grey area in China.

  10. 10

    Apple Translate or Microsoft Translator, plus Pleco

    Set up at homeGood extra
    Google Translate is blocked online in China; its offline Chinese pack works only if you download it before you go. Apple Translate is free, works fully offline once Chinese is downloaded and translates through the camera; on Android, Microsoft Translator works without a VPN and has offline packs too. I would also add Pleco, a free offline Chinese dictionary: when the translation of a menu line makes no sense, look up the characters one by one. Its camera recognition is a paid add-on (€9.99 on the Irish App Store). Alipay also has Scan, Translate built in for menus and signs.

  11. 11

    RedNote (Xiaohongshu): where locals actually find places

    Set up at homeGood extra
    RedNote is where a lot of people in China look up where to eat and what is worth the queue: new cafés, viewpoints, food streets. It added AI translation in January 2025 and you can sign up with a foreign number, but the interface is still mostly Chinese, so search with the Chinese name of a place copied from AMap.

  12. 12

    Dianping: reviews, plus a digital queue number

    On arrival is fineGood extra
    China's main restaurant review app. The App Store lists English, though in practice the English is partial and reviews are not translated. The reason to install it is 排队 (queue): take a number for a busy restaurant from your phone and watch how many tables are ahead of you, instead of standing at the door for an hour.

Already inside Alipay (nothing to download)

A lot of apps on other lists do not need downloading, because they already run inside Alipay. Open them from Alipay's search bar or the Transport section, and they use the card you already linked.

  • Taxi. Transport, then Taxi, opens DiDi inside Alipay, with no separate account and fewer payment problems. The standalone DiDi app also has English and in-app translation if you prefer it.
  • Metro code. Transport, then your city: activate each city's metro QR code once and scan through the gates.
  • Shared bikes. Scan the QR code on a Hello Bike or Meituan bike with Alipay's scanner and the right mini-program opens. Meituan's own app needs a Chinese number; this way does not.
  • Power banks. Scan a rental kiosk (Energy Monster and others). Expect a ¥99 hold on your card that is released when you return it.
  • Food delivery. Ele.me was renamed Taobao Instant Commerce in December 2025 and runs as a mini-program in Alipay, which is the easiest delivery option for visitors.
  • Coffee. Luckin's own app wants a Chinese number; the Luckin mini-program in Alipay does not.
  • Luggage storage. Search 行李寄存 (luggage storage) to find left-luggage points near stations and sights.
  • China Bound. China Daily's English travel platform, launched 1 January 2026, with translation, tickets, hotels and an AI itinerary helper. I have not tested it yet.
  • Shanghai Easy Go. Shanghai's visitor service inside Alipay since July 2025: about 30 mini-programs for food, transport, tickets, luggage and tax refunds, with translation in 14 languages.
  • Travel Wallet. Launched in April 2026 with Beijing's GO BEIJING service: you fund it by an international transfer and spend anywhere in mainland China with no card fee. It is open to people from 40 countries and regions, so check yours is on the list before relying on it.

China travel apps you can skip

  • Google Maps. Blocked without a VPN, and even with one the China map is offset, so your blue dot can sit hundreds of metres from where you are.
  • Uber. It sold its China business to DiDi in 2016.
  • Airbnb for mainland China. It removed its mainland listings in July 2022.
  • Baidu Maps. Works, but AMap Global is the better English choice.
  • The standalone Meituan and Luckin apps. Both expect a Chinese number; use their Alipay mini-programs.
  • Alipay Tour Card. A prepaid card with a 5% top-up fee, and 2026 reports say it closed to new users. Do not plan around it.
  • Waygo. Still in the App Store, but not updated since 2017.
  • LetsVPN and free VPNs. LetsVPN left mainland China in April 2026; free VPNs are reported to fail.
  • Lookalike 12306 apps. Only 铁路12306 from China Railway is official.
